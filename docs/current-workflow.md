# Current Operations Workflow  
### Demo-Driven Direct-Sales Distributor Model

## Overview

This document maps the typical operational workflow of a small regional distributor that sells products through in-home demonstrations and services existing customers through repair and maintenance appointments.

The workflow relies heavily on manual processes including phone calls, paper forms, and informal scheduling methods. These operational patterns create friction in lead handling, scheduling efficiency, and customer lifecycle management.

---

# Lead Generation

Potential customers typically enter the system through several channels:

- Home and trade shows  
- Referrals from existing customers  
- Local advertising  
- Inbound phone inquiries  

These sources create inbound interest but often lack structured digital capture mechanisms.

---

# Lead Capture

When a potential customer expresses interest, contact information is collected.

Typical information collected:

- Name  
- Phone number  
- Address  
- Interest in a demonstration  

Collection methods may include:

- Paper forms at events  
- Phone conversations recorded manually  
- Informal notes

Because intake is not standardized, lead data may be incomplete or inconsistent.

---

# Lead Recording

Captured lead information is stored manually.

Common storage methods:

- Paper forms  
- Notebooks  
- Basic spreadsheets  

Without a centralized database or CRM system, leads may be difficult to track and follow consistently.

---

# Demo Scheduling

The primary sales activity is the in-home demonstration.

Typical scheduling workflow:

1. Staff contacts the potential customer by phone  
2. Availability is discussed  
3. Appointment is scheduled manually  
4. Appointment is recorded in a personal or shared calendar  

Challenges may include:

- Delayed responses  
- Scheduling conflicts  
- Inefficient travel planning

---

# In-Home Demonstration

A sales representative travels to the customer’s home to perform a product demonstration.

During the demonstration:

- Product features are presented  
- Product performance is shown  
- Customer questions are answered  

This stage represents the primary opportunity to convert the lead into a sale.

---

# Sales Outcome

Following the demonstration, several outcomes are possible.

## Purchase

If the customer decides to purchase:

- Payment is collected  
- Product ordering or delivery is arranged  
- Ownership of the product begins  

## Consideration

If the customer is undecided:

- Follow-up may occur at a later date  
- Outreach is typically informal and inconsistent  

## No Purchase

If the customer declines:

- The lead may not be tracked further  
- Future marketing or outreach is uncommon  

This stage represents a common point where potential revenue opportunities are lost due to lack of structured follow-up.

---

# Customer Lifecycle

Customers who purchase products may later require:

- Accessories or replacement parts  
- Maintenance services  
- Repair services  

However, without structured customer records, these needs are often handled reactively.

---

# Service and Repair Intake

When a customer requires service, the workflow typically begins with a phone call.

Typical steps include:

1. Customer calls to describe an issue  
2. Staff records the issue informally  
3. A service appointment is scheduled  
4. A technician visits the customer location  

Challenges may include:

- Lack of service history tracking  
- Inconsistent appointment documentation  
- Limited visibility into repeat service patterns

---

# Summary of Operational Friction

Several structural weaknesses are present in the current workflow.

## Lead Management

- Manual lead capture  
- No centralized lead database  
- Potential for lost leads

## Scheduling

- Phone-based scheduling  
- No automated reminders  
- Inefficient route planning

## Sales Tracking

- No structured sales pipeline  
- Limited visibility into conversion rates

## Customer Lifecycle

- Inconsistent follow-up  
- No automated service reminders  
- Limited repeat-purchase engagement

---

# Transition Opportunity

These operational gaps create an opportunity to introduce structured automation including:

- Digital lead intake  
- AI-assisted lead classification  
- CRM-based lead tracking  
- Automated demo scheduling  
- Customer follow-up workflows  
- Service ticket management
